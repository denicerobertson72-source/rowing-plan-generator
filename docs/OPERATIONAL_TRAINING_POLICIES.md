# Operational Training Policies

Polarized Periodized Rowing Plan | R9F.5a.4b | v1.0 | 2026-11-26

**Status: all 35 policies approved for pilot, not yet implemented.** Companion to `TRAINING_POLICY_FRAMEWORK.md`, `SCIENTIFIC_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md` and `CROSS_POLICY_CONFLICT_REVIEW.md`. The foundational framework's pending-numerical-policy statements are superseded only for the specific parameters approved here. Other safeguards remain in force.

## Interpretation and common contract

All ranges below are configurable candidate planning guidance, not mandatory quotas, guarantees of safety, or scientifically demonstrated optimal values for masters rowers. Research rationale is indexed by S1–S10 in the evidence review. Every policy record includes scope, decision, exceptions and an observable acceptance condition. Policy IDs are stable; version any parameter changes. Apply authority, restrictions and evidence quality before defaults. Preserve original, modified and actual records; never treat missing evidence as zero or confirmed recovery. Distinguish prospective recommendations from accepted prescriptions. No medical clearance, invented fatigue score, or automatic demographic multiplier.

## S1–S3: Dose, recovery and periodization

### OP-01 — Volume progression (F1–14; S1)
Consider adding 5–10 minutes to an easy endurance session and approximately 2–5% to comparable established weekly physical volume when justified by goals and tolerance. Independently evaluate rowing, individual modalities, strength, quality and frequency changes; no automatic weekly compounding. Maintain a new dose until evidence supports further progression. Exceptions require justification and appropriate authority. No exact category-specific percentage cap was approved. **Acceptance:** unchanged total volume with rowing rising 240 to 335 minutes is flagged for independent review; capacity alone cannot authorize growth.

### OP-02 — Loading cycles (F15–23; S2–S3)
Candidate loading:consolidation templates: 2:1 for unfamiliar stimulus; 3:1 for established consistent athletes; 4:1 with demonstrated tolerance of longer cycles; flexible for interruptions or competition clusters. A 1:1 structure was discussed as a possible special-case illustration, not a separately approved default. No age-only selection. **Acceptance:** cycle timing adapts to actual response and competitions, with reason recorded.

### OP-03 — Consolidation and recovery (F15–23; S2–S3)
Candidate reductions relative to comparable established physical dose: composition-focused consolidation 0–10%; ordinary consolidation 10–25%; more substantial recovery 25–40% or individualized. Reduce relevant interval, muscular or neuromuscular demand even when minutes remain unchanged. Greater reduction or rest is permitted; race and medical pathways are separate. **Acceptance:** no forced reallocation of removed minutes to meet a weekly target; distinguish consolidation from recovery.

### OP-04 — Resuming loading (F9,15–23; S3)
Ordinarily return to an appropriate previously tolerated dose after consolidation before further progression. Do not automatically exceed the previous high or use the reduced week as a new baseline. **Acceptance:** 520 → 420 consolidation → up to previously tolerated 520, subject to response and constraints, rather than automatic progression above it.

### OP-05 — Developmental block durations (F8,11,16,40; S3)
Provisional planning/review ranges (weeks): foundation 4–8; aerobic base 6–12; aerobic capacity 6–8; aerobic strength endurance 6–8; threshold 4–8; anaerobic 4–6; peak power 4–6; maximal strength 6–12; sprint-specific 4–6; head-race-specific 4–8; technique ongoing; recovery/transition contextual. Review around four weeks and block boundaries; actual exposure and response govern changes. **Acceptance:** no automatic claim of adaptation at calendar end; interrupted block may extend, repeat, shorten or change.

### OP-06 — Minimal progression evidence (F4,5,9–13,47; S1–S3)
Periodic athlete confirmation of reasonably consistent completion, appropriate challenge or easier-than-expected response, adequate recovery and absence of significant interruption/restriction may support progression without detailed logs. Contradictions require review; missing confirmation cannot itself authorize increase. **Acceptance:** minimal-log athlete can progress with credible confirmation; missing logs alone do not imply rest or tolerance.

## S4–S6: Composition, strength and intensity

### OP-07 — Strength frequency/duration (F24–26,33; S4)
| Context | Sessions/week | Minutes/session |
|---|---:|---:|
| New/foundation | 1–2 | 20–40 |
| General preparation | 2–3 | 30–60 |
| Strength-focused | 2–3 | 40–60 |
| Specific preparation | 1–3 | 25–50 |
| Competition season | 1–2 | 20–45 |
| Consolidation | 1–2 | 20–40 |
| Taper/recovery | Individualized | Individualized |
Earlier illustrative taper 0–2 sessions is not a mandatory minimum or maximum. Honor opt-out, experience, fixed external program and available days. **Acceptance:** prohibited days never receive strength; frequency can fall below template if justified.

### OP-08 — Strength types (F25–26; S4)
Foundational (goblet squat, row, hinge); maximal strength (squat, leg press, bench pull); hypertrophy (compound/accessory work); explosive (appropriate medicine-ball throws, jumps); strength endurance (controlled repeated resistance); maintenance (familiar movements, reduced demand); reduced-demand (lighter/reduced-volume familiar work); stability/mobility (trunk and shoulder stability, hip mobility). Prescribe type, purpose, duration, examples and general execution guidance, not individual loads or a full lifting program. Distinguish mobility from substantial muscular demand. **Acceptance:** mobility does not automatically count as heavy strength; unknown muscular demand remains unknown.

### OP-09 — Concurrent training (F27–29,33; S4)
Respect day-specific preferences and explicit permission for demanding doubles. Easy endurance plus strength can be combined; demanding endurance with substantial or explosive strength preferably separated, with at least three hours a soft preference where relevant and known feasible. Prioritize the key developmental stimulus when order is flexible. No assumed time separation from date-only data; retain existing hard guards until validated replacement. **Acceptance:** consent alone cannot bypass sequence restrictions; unknown time cannot satisfy a three-hour preference.

### OP-10 — Seasonal category allocation (F6,25,30–32; S5)
| Phase | Rowing % | Strength % | General aerobic % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| General prep | 50–70 | 20–30 | 10–25 |
| Specific prep | 60–80 | 15–25 | 0–20 |
| Competition prep | 70–85 | 10–20 | 0–15 |
| Competition | 70–90 | 10–20 | 0–15 |
| Transition | 35–65 | 10–25 | 15–40 |
Denominator is physical minutes allocated among these three categories; separately report any complementary minutes and total physical time. Select coherent shares totaling 100% within that denominator when feasible. Envelopes yield to authority, category tolerance, recovery and commitments. No compulsory cross-training or automatic transfer into rowing. **Acceptance:** opt-out produces zero prescribed cross-training despite template; unexplained dose/category growth prohibited.

### OP-11 — Modality substitutions (F30–31,43; S5)
Prefer rowing-to-rowing substitution for rowing-specific purpose, then authorized aerobic alternative when appropriate. Winter standalone cross-training, supplemental cardio and temporary substitution permissions remain distinct. Preserve intended stimulus and report specificity losses; technique/crew work is not replaceable by generic cardio. No universal duration, power or HR conversion. **Acceptance:** weather substitution does not regenerate the entire plan or silently authorize standalone cross-training.

### OP-12 — Intensity distribution (F6,8,31–32; S6)
Predominantly low-intensity endurance; support pyramidal general preparation and greater polarization or targeted mixed distributions when appropriate. Foundation strongly easy; threshold block targeted moderate work; sprint/head race emphasis individualized; consolidation reduces relevant demanding work. No fixed 80/20 requirement or approved universal numeric phase TID. Seven rowing bands map provisionally to physiological domains, not measured thresholds: UT3/UT2 usually low, UT1 may cross first threshold, AT near second, TR/AN high work; PP separately characterize neuromuscular exposure. **Acceptance:** plan not rejected solely for missing arbitrary 80/20 share.

### OP-13 — Intensity accounting (F17,18,32,49; S6)
Separate all physical minutes by category; classifiable endurance session-goal counts; rowing intensity minutes; all endurance intensity minutes. Separate prescribed vs actual, measured HR vs power vs intended work, and unknown coverage. Strength excluded from endurance denominator, included in physical and muscular demand. Warm-up/cooldown and active recovery classified if known; passive rest separately identified, never invented easy work. **Acceptance:** 500 physical = 300 rowing +135 strength +65 aerobic; 30 high minutes /365 endurance = 8.2%, not 20% merely because 1 of 5 sessions is hard. Unknowns explicit; reconcile minute totals exactly.

### OP-14 — Quality frequency (F8,17,28,32; S6)
Candidate weekly quality exposures by endurance-session count: 2–3 → 0–1; 4–5 → 1–2; 6–7 → 1–2; 8–10+ → 1–3. No default established for fewer than two sessions; contextual review. Quality may include UT1, AT, TR, AN, PP; not all quality is high intensity. Include demanding races, practices and assessments without duplicate credit. **Acceptance:** two maximal races may satisfy planned quality frequency; no automatic extra intervals.

## S7–S8: Competition and interruption

### OP-15 — Taper templates (F34–35,38; S7)
| Priority | Days | Endurance-volume reduction |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 7–14, potentially up to 21 | 40–60% |
| B | 3–7 | 20–40% |
| C | 0–3 | 0–20% |
Reference comparable pre-taper endurance dose, not availability or already reduced consolidation week. A range informed by endurance meta-analysis; B/C coaching defaults. **Acceptance:** taper not compounded onto consolidation by default; no mandatory taper for C.

### OP-16 — Taper composition (F35; S7)
Retain selected race-specific intensity while reducing repetitions, demanding work, endurance duration and muscular demand. Preserve frequency and skills where practical; avoid unnecessary tests and automatic pre-taper overload. **Acceptance:** high-intensity work minutes decline even when target intensity retained.

### OP-17 — Competition episodes (F34,36; S7)
Track individual event date, distance, boat class, priority, effort, duration and optional warm-up/cooldown, between-race timing, travel and response within regatta episode. Require basic info only, permit provisional entries. **Acceptance:** multiple events remain distinct; episode is grouping not extra physical load; unknown duration not zero.

### OP-18 — Protected race recovery (F34,36–37; S7)
| Exposure | Initial protected days after final event |
|---|---:|
| Controlled developmental | 0–1 |
| Single maximal sprint | 1–2 |
| Single maximal head race | 1–3 |
| Multiple maximal sprints | 1–3 |
| Multiple maximal head races | 2–4 |
| Demanding multi-day | 2–5 or individualized |
These are coaching guardrails, not recovery kinetics. Ordinarily restrict new demanding quality, substantial strength and maximal tests during protection; consider easy technical work/rest. Review between races and after episode; expiry not proof of recovery. Unknown exposure uses conservative fallback. Existing hard A/B/C 3/2/1, soft 5/3/2 and multiple-event rule remain until reviewed replacement. **Acceptance:** maximal C head-race weekend can warrant greater protection than controlled A event; legacy rules remain during shadow evaluation.

### OP-19 — Competition clusters (F38; S7)
Review competitions within seven days as connected; 8–14 days for interactions; also inspect overlapping preparation/recovery outside these windows. Protect principal race while using other races as possible quality stimuli. **Acceptance:** overlapping windows produce one coherent schedule, not stacked independent tapers or repeated quality debt.

### OP-20 — Temporary availability (F39,42; S8)
Dated temporary exceptions for duration, per-day availability, equipment and modalities; expire automatically; preserve permanent profile. **Acceptance:** vacation with no available modalities permits zero prescribed exercise; post-vacation availability reverts without automatic volume rebound.

### OP-21 — Ordinary inactivity restoration (F39–42; S8)
Candidate first-week physical dose relative to previously tolerated volume: up to 7 days 80–100% where appropriate; 8–14 days 60–80%; 15–28 days 40–60%; over 28 days individualized provisional baseline. 1–3 days generally retain plan; 4–7 may use familiar reduced-demand sessions. Consider actual alternative activity and category exposure; defer unfamiliar/demanding work. Restoration is distinct from 2–5% new progression; no fixed automatic restoration ramp. Not for illness/injury. **Acceptance:** active hiking trip is not treated as total inactivity; no compensatory harder workouts.

### OP-22 — Health-related return (F41,48; S8)
States: suspended; restricted participation; initial return; progressive restoration; ordinary training. May move backward. Status, explicit restrictions, response and appropriate professional guidance govern progression; no medical clearance or automatic date-based advancement. Concerning symptoms such as chest discomfort, abnormal breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations prompt stopping and seeking medical evaluation; severe/urgent symptoms require urgent care. No generic percentages from OP-21. **Acceptance:** competition cannot override restriction; absence of feedback cannot advance state.

### OP-23 — Interrupted blocks (F13,40; S8)
Continue, extend, repeat, shorten or replace based on completed developmental exposure, season and upcoming competitions. No training debt or compressed catch-up. **Acceptance:** two weeks illness before A race triggers achievable-goal review rather than doubled quality sessions.

## S9–S10: Targets and assessments

### OP-24 — Individualized bands (F49–50; S9)
Retain UT3/UT2/UT1/AT/TR/AN/PP. Use relevant measured assessments, authorized coach/athlete targets, comparable history and conservative estimates; distinguish source, modality, protocol and uncertainty. No automatic overwrite of established HR bands from new 2k. **Acceptance:** target revisions proposed with provenance and appropriate authority.

### OP-25 — Execution measures (F49–50,53; S9)
UT3/UT2 HR and effort, sustainable power; UT1 HR/power/split/rate; AT power/split, completion and HR response; TR power/split/duration and HR response; AN power/work duration and repetition quality; PP peak power/force and execution, HR monitoring only. Adapt to available equipment, modality and athlete. **Acceptance:** short peak-power effort never requires reaching target HR; average HR alone cannot establish time in band.

### OP-26 — Modality-specific targets (F30–31,49; S9)
Separate erg, on-water and other endurance modality targets. Transfer only provisionally, with field review. No GPS-to-Concept2 watts; no universal threshold HR equality. **Acceptance:** on-water speed history cannot overwrite erg power targets.

### OP-27 — Longitudinal monitoring (F10–11,49–50,52; S9)
Compare sufficiently similar duration, intensity, protocol, rate, conditions, HR/power/split and effort. Optional HR drift or power/HR trends, no universal 5% pass/fail and no inference of recovery alone. Basic completion, standard summary and detailed logging all supported. Preserve measured/reported/derived/estimated distinction. **Acceptance:** average HR only produces no fabricated trace; improvement described as observed performance not VO2max change.

### OP-28 — Repeated mismatch triggers (F2–3,11,49–50; S9)
One comparable mismatch: feedback; two or more: flag potential pattern; three or more consistent: consider targeted review or adjustment; persistent after adjustment: investigate dose, target and developmental context. Configurable review triggers only. Use daily sequencing, 7/14-day patterns, ~28-day baseline, 8–12-week consistency. Immediate restrictions are exceptions. **Acceptance:** three hot-weather sessions do not automatically alter bands; no change from unsupported noncomparable data.

### OP-29 — Assessment menu (F51; S10)
Core optional erg protocols: seven-stroke peak, 60-second maximal average, 2k maximal, maximal 30R20. Additional 20-second, longer distance, submaximal, threshold, validated critical-power and lab tests when relevant. Maximal and submaximal 30R20 distinct. **Acceptance:** no mandatory full battery; no 30R20-derived direct LT, CP or VO2max.

### OP-30 — Interpretation (F5,8,13,51; S10)
Assess specific capabilities and integrate goals/season/response; no fixed ratio diagnosis of physiological limiter. **Acceptance:** same evidence may support different recommendations for sprint vs head race; direct performance vs proxy marked explicitly.

### OP-31 — Variability (F50–52; S10)
Consider protocol reliability, repeated comparisons, equipment, conditions and familiarization. No universal meaningful-change threshold. **Acceptance:** one-second 2k improvement is reported without automatic target reset or definitive adaptation claim.

### OP-32 — On-water assessments (F31,51; S10)
First-class modality history: boat class, scull/sweep, crew, course/direction, GPS or water speed, wind/current, HR, stroke rate, power optional, rigging, conditions and interruptions. Opposite-direction comparable velocity averages may reduce stable current effect, not guarantee correction. **Acceptance:** separate histories; crew outcome not attributed entirely to one individual.

### OP-33 — Reassessment review (F11,51; S10)
Candidate opportunities: submaximal 1–2 weeks; seven-stroke 4–8; 20/60-second 6–8; 2k 8–12; maximal 30R20 6–12; on-water/lab contextual. Review not mandatory testing; recommend only decision-relevant tests, athlete/coach requests separate. Defer if restriction or demanding context. **Acceptance:** elapsed interval alone does not generate maximal test.

### OP-34 — Assessment scheduling (F32,51,54; S10)
Support athlete, planner and coach/team scheduled tests, fixed or flexible dates, multi-day episodes. Account for actual warm-up/work/recovery/cooldown; replace compatible quality, not add by default. Respect restrictions and conflicts; accept/defer/decline optional recommendations. **Acceptance:** fixed team test protected in calendar but marked conflict if medically restricted; no duplicate minutes or quality.

### OP-35 — History and freshness (F4,51–54; S10)
Stable unique assessment/episode IDs, date, protocol/version, modality/equipment, maximal/submaximal intent, rate restrictions, measured and derived metrics, conditions, source. Append repeated tests; preserve old records and contextual relevance, no universal expiry. **Acceptance:** repeat same protocol and partial episode updates preserve previous tests and IDs; verify persistence before winter testing.
