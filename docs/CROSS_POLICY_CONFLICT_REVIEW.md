# Cross-Policy Conflict Review

Polarized Periodized Rowing Plan | R9F.5a.4c | v1.0 | 2026-11-26

**Status:** design-level review completed; proposed resolution details pending owner approval and repository audit. No code or production behavior changed. Based on the approved 55 foundational and 35 operational policies and prior diagnostic discussion, not direct inspection of the repository.

## 1. Precedence and decision contract

1. Explicit health restrictions, hard calendar/availability constraints and existing validated guards are non-negotiable within their applicable scope. A conflicting fixed commitment remains visible with a conflict, not a prescription to violate a restriction.
2. Athlete/coach authority: permissions, fixed commitments, autonomy, readiness-data consent, modalities, doubles, rest preferences. These are separate controls; one cannot imply another. Hard constraints outrank ordinary overrides.
3. Current context: health return, competition/assessment protection, actual recent exposure, interruption and recovery. Resolve overlapping contexts jointly; do not blindly multiply reductions.
4. Developmental objectives and competition priorities: protect key stimulus if feasible, maintain complementary capabilities; no catch-up debt.
5. Sustainable dose and category tolerance: capacity ceiling, established exposure, response, developmental and recovery-adjusted dose; candidate templates are flexible.
6. Composition and scheduling: choose authorized categories, modalities, timing and purpose; feasibility failures return upstream for a revised candidate, not a fabricated physiological conclusion.
7. Execution targets and adaptation: retain provenance, use comparable evidence, apply permissions and request approval when necessary. Preserve original and actual history.

Precedence is not an inflexible linear pipeline. Later infeasibility can require reconsideration of earlier choices, but cannot weaken hard restrictions. Proposals must show source policy IDs, evidence limitations, baseline reference, before/after doses, conflicts, authority, changes and validation results. No default may silently become a hard constraint.

## 4. Repository audit priorities and staging

Known issues below are from prior diagnostic discussion, not newly reproduced. Audit read-only first.

- Assessment persistence: verify append vs overwrite, stable IDs and episode partial updates, field round trips and effect of new tests on plan regeneration. Resolve before new winter tests.
- R2 dose: distinguish capacity/established/tolerance/developmental/recovery-adjusted/feasible; currently reported 500-minute scenario may hide category growth.
- R3/R4: strength prohibited Saturday violation observed in prior diagnostic; day-specific modality and authority constraints; preserve shared sequence feasibility and rollback.
- R5: keep component-level exact duration and one-to-one identity; no duplicate histories or prospective activity mislabeled actual.
- R6: legacy quality envelope infeasibility and old ratio limiter logic; do not enable new quality without validated materialization and accounting.
- Existing guards: strength/quality same/next-day, race hard A/B/C 3/2/1 and soft 5/3/2, multi-race additional protection, legacy subtype timing. Inventory exact scope before replacement; shadow comparison required.
- Privacy: readiness not automatically consumed today; any authorized expansion requires separate consent, provenance and tests.

## 5. Validation gates

For every OP and conflict ID: identify implementation owner, source file/symbol, current behavior, expected behavior, evidence/authority and test fixture. Require pure policy tests, cross-calendar history continuity, privacy and override tests, integration conservation, regression tests and production diagnostic comparison. Include minimal-log, new rower with endurance history, established masters, cross-training opt-out, coach fixed assessment, competition cluster, interruption, health restriction and infeasible schedule cases. Verify no unintended changes to normal generation until acceptance. Record unresolved infeasibility explicitly; never force successful output by dropping hard constraints. Pilot observations are not clinical validation or proof of efficacy.

## Conclusion

No fundamental contradiction requires reopening any of the 90 approved policies. The main risks are translating flexible templates into hard constraints, conflating evidence and authority, overlapping recovery adjustments, and losing historical continuity. The proposed precedence and conflict scenarios should be approved as design guidance before Codex audit; detailed algorithm choices remain pending. No code has been changed.
