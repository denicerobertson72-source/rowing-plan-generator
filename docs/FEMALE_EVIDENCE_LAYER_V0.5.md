# Female-focused evidence layer v0.5 — 2026 policy update

The app is female-evidence-first and athlete-specific. It contains no sex,
menstrual-cycle, hormonal-contraception, menopause, or age multiplier. Actual
2k remains the primary performance anchor; 60-second power is the stronger
default short-duration input; seven-stroke peak supports starts and PP work but
a small single reading cannot alter a plan.

## Evidence scope

S031 and S032 are the 2026 NSCA peer-reviewed position statements by Dabbs et
al. They are high-priority consensus evidence. The statements used evidence
from competitive female athletes at tier 2 and above whenever possible and
define their own population and scope. This supports careful applicability; it
does not make every conclusion universal to every athlete, nor does it exclude
athletes outside that scope from using the app.

## Policy hierarchy

Programming is primarily derived from athlete needs analysis, sport/race
demands, individual dosing tolerance, completed training, and rigorous
monitoring (S032). Sex-specific physiology can be relevant context and can
affect individual needs or the magnitude/rate of adaptation. Basic
strength-and-conditioning principles, including progressive overload, remain
applicable across humans (S032). The app therefore uses individual evidence
and response rather than categorical assumptions.

| Topic | Research/position-statement supported | Evidence-informed app policy | Not supported / not implemented |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Programming | Needs analysis, sport demands, individual tolerance and monitoring (S032) | Completed work, tests, race context, availability and explicit preferences inform plans | A sex-category adjustment to volume, intensity, recovery, or performance |
| Menstrual cycle | Symptoms may matter to readiness, sleep, recovery and training quality; current evidence does not establish a consistent phase-performance relationship sufficient for phase-based resistance prescription for most active female athletes (S032) | Optional, athlete-reported symptoms/readiness may support a future review workflow | Follicular/luteal multiplier, phase-based periodization, required tracking, or treating missing data as abnormal |
| Hormonal contraception | Individual effects are heterogeneous; cycle-phase frameworks do not cleanly apply across contraceptive use (S032) | Respond only to an observed athlete response | Automatic adjustment for pills, IUDs, implants, injections, patches, rings, or non-use |
| Life stage and health | Lifespan, injury and health context can warrant informed, individualized consideration (S031) | Strength, muscle/bone health, aerobic conditioning, recovery, symptoms and tolerance remain relevant context | Menopause/age multiplier, automatic lower load, extra rest day, altered HR zones, or diagnosis/risk score |
| Strength continuity | Resistance/strength conditioning is an important training dimension across the lifespan (S031, S032) | Rolling individualized strength targets should not casually disappear as endurance load rises; taper/recovery changes remain individually constrained | A universal female lifting frequency or fixed weekly strength requirement |
| RED-S, bone health, pelvic floor, injury | These are health/injury considerations in the position statement (S031) | Explicit athlete report, clinician restriction, coach/athlete decision, and later optional readiness context can inform review | RED-S diagnosis, low-energy-availability inference, bone-risk score, or automatic restriction from age, body size, menstrual status, or menopause |
| Pregnancy/postpartum | Distinct lifespan conditions needing specific considerations (S031) | Future support must use explicit context and appropriate clinical guidance | Automatic pregnancy/postpartum programming or inference from profile data |

## Monitoring and privacy boundary

Female-health, contraception, life-stage, HRV, iron, RED-S, injury, sleep and
readiness data do not automatically alter training. Tracking is optional; no
athlete must disclose sensitive information. Reported state may support a
future athlete/coach/clinician-guided response, but the training app does not
diagnose medical conditions or replace clinical care.

Completed as planned is low-burden monitoring: it records that the athlete
reports completing the prescription. It does **not** establish low RPE,
successful target watts, normal HR, absence of symptoms, or excellent recovery.
Those require separately reported data.

## Rowing-specific application

ACL, concussion, breast injury, patellofemoral pain, bone stress, and pelvic
floor issues should not create generic rowing rules merely because they appear
in a broad position statement. For this app, reported symptoms or clinician
restrictions are individual context; fixed medical restrictions override
performance optimization. Course/race, completed load, and recovery logic
remain separate from health diagnosis.

Sources: S031 (Part I: lifespan, injury and health) and S032 (Part II:
adaptation and program design). The app preserves the prior non-directive
female-health boundary rather than extrapolating a medical questionnaire or
automatic programming system.
